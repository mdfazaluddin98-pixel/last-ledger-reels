London Was Drowned in Beer

On October 17, 1814, a massive beer vat inside London’s Meux Brewery suddenly ruptured.

Within seconds, more than half a million gallons of beer surged into the streets of St. Giles.

The wave smashed through buildings, flooded basement homes, and killed eight people.

But the disaster had an even darker ending.

The brewery went to court. The jury declared the flood an “act of God.” No one was held responsible, and the brewery continued operating.

A disaster that lasted only minutes left families facing consequences for years.

The Last Ledger explores the strange, shocking, and forgotten events that shaped human history.
