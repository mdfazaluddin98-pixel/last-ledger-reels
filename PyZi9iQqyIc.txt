How English Archers Made Knights Useless in One Afternoon

In thirteen forty-six, thousands of French knights rode onto the battlefield expecting victory. Instead, English and Welsh longbowmen turned the French attack into one of the most devastating defeats of the Hundred Years’ War.

Edward the Third used high ground, defensive obstacles, disciplined formations, and relentless longbow fire to exploit the weaknesses of heavily armored cavalry. When the rain weakened the Genoese crossbows and French attacks began collapsing, the battlefield became a trap.

From charging warhorses and waves of arrows to the doomed final charge of the blind King John of Bohemia, the Battle of Cressy revealed a terrifying truth: armor, noble blood, and courage could not guarantee victory against preparation and superior tactics.

This is the story of how ordinary archers helped shatter the confidence of Europe's warrior elite.
