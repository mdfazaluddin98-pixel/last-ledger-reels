Rome's Emperor Tried to Replace the Senate With a Horse

A Roman emperor. A favorite horse. And a plan that could humiliate the most powerful men in Rome.

According to the ancient historian Suetonius, Caligula wanted his horse, Incitatus, to become a consul—the highest office in the Roman government. But was it a serious plan, or the ultimate insult to the Roman Senate?

Incitatus lived like an aristocrat, with a marble stable, servants, expensive food, and reportedly invitations to dine with important Romans. The story reveals what happened when one ruler became so powerful that even a ridiculous idea could terrify an entire empire.

In A.D. forty-one, Caligula was assassinated by members of his own guard and court. The horse never became consul. But the story survived because it exposed something deeper than madness: what happens when nobody can safely tell a ruler no.
