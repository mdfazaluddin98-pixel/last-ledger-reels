He Was Rome’s Emperor—Then He Entered the Gladiator Arena

In the Roman Empire, one emperor wanted more than absolute power. Commodus wanted to become a gladiator. He entered the arena, fought staged opponents, killed animals, and demanded to be treated like Hercules. But while he chased the applause of the crowd, his court grew terrified of him.

In A.D. 192, his mistress Marcia discovered that Commodus had planned to have her executed. She poisoned him, but the attempt failed. A wrestler named Narcissus was then sent into the emperor’s bath—and strangled him.

After his death, the Senate condemned Commodus’s memory. His statues were destroyed, and his name was removed from public records.

The emperor who wanted immortal glory became a warning about power, obsession, and the price of turning government into a performance.
