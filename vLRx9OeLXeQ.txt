They Sent Machine Guns to Kill Birds. The Birds Won

In 1932, Australia sent the army to war against 20,000 emus. Machine guns, combat veterans, thousands of rounds of ammunition — against birds. The birds won.

After World War I, soldiers were given land in Western Australia to farm wheat. Then the emus arrived — 20,000 of them, destroying crops and smashing fences. The government's answer was the military. Major Meredith arrived with two Lewis machine guns, ready for what looked like a massacre.

It wasn't. The emus scattered, outran trucks, jammed the guns just by existing, and after days of failure and public mockery, the Australian military withdrew. A modern army with machine guns had been defeated by birds.

This is the true, absurd story of the Great Emu War of 1932.
