Julius Caesar Was Captured by Pirates—Then He Crucified Every One

In seventy-five B.C., Julius Caesar was captured by pirates who demanded a ransom. But instead of begging for his freedom, Caesar laughed at their demands—and ordered them to ask for even more money.

While the pirates held him prisoner, Caesar treated them like servants, read them his poetry, and warned them that he would return and crucify them.

They laughed.

Then Caesar was released.

Most men would have escaped and forgotten the pirates. Caesar raised a fleet, hunted them down, and returned to collect the debt.

Discover the shocking story of the kidnapping that became one of history’s most famous acts of revenge.

The pirates thought they had captured a helpless prisoner. They had actually captured the man who would come back for them.
