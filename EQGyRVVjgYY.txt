Rome’s Final Emperor: The Day an Empire Lost Its Throne

He was the emperor of Rome. He was also a teenager—and an army was coming to take his throne.

In 476 AD, Romulus Augustulus became the last recognized emperor of the Western Roman Empire. But by the time he inherited the throne, Rome was already weakened by civil wars, political instability, and powerful military commanders.

When Odoacer marched toward Ravenna, the young emperor could not stop him. Romulus was captured, forced to surrender, and removed from power.

There was no final battle. No dramatic last stand. No burning of Rome.

Just a teenager losing an empire that had ruled much of the known world.

But 476 AD did not mean that all Rome disappeared. The Eastern Roman Empire continued for almost another thousand years, while Roman law, government, language, and culture continued to shape history.

So what really ended in 476?

And how did one of the greatest empires in history lose its final emperor?

The Last Ledger explores the people, places, ideas, and moments that shaped human history.
