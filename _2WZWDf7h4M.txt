He Destroyed Jerusalem. Then Rome Called Him a Good Emperor.

He destroyed Jerusalem. Then he became one of Rome’s most respected emperors.

In AD 70, Titus commanded the Roman army during the siege and destruction of Jerusalem. The Second Temple was destroyed, and the consequences changed Jewish history forever.

But Titus’s story did not end with conquest.

Years later, he became emperor of Rome. During his short reign, Mount Vesuvius erupted, Pompeii was buried, and Rome suffered devastating disasters. Ancient historians remembered Titus for his generosity and his efforts to help survivors.

How could the same man be remembered as both the destroyer of a holy city and a merciful emperor?

This short documentary explores the rise, military victories, disasters, and complicated legacy of Titus.
