They Put a Dead Pope on Trial

In 897 AD, Rome witnessed one of the strangest trials in history.

Pope Formosus had been dead for months when his successor ordered his corpse exhumed, dressed in papal robes, and placed on a throne before a church court.

A deacon was forced to answer questions for the dead man.

The verdict was already decided.

Guilty.

The corpse was stripped, its blessing fingers were cut off, and its body was thrown into the Tiber River.

But this horrifying trial was never really about justice.

It was about power.

And within months, the pope who ordered it was overthrown and imprisoned.

The man who tried to prove his power was absolute discovered how quickly power could disappear.

The Last Ledger explores the people, places, ideas, and moments that shaped human history.
