Europe’s Darkest Year: When the Sun Disappeared

In AD 536, the world became strangely dark.

Across parts of Europe and the Mediterranean, the sun appeared weak, temperatures fell, and harvests began to fail. Summer seemed to disappear.

But what caused it?

Scientists believe powerful volcanic eruptions sent enormous amounts of ash and sulfur into the atmosphere, blocking sunlight and cooling the planet. The people living through it had no way to understand the disaster unfolding above them.

Then came famine. Years later, another catastrophe struck: the Plague of Justinian.

For a generation, people faced a terrifying combination of darkness, cold, hunger, and disease.

Was AD 536 the darkest year in human history?

This documentary explores the mysterious climate disaster that changed the sixth century—and reveals how ordinary people struggled to survive when the sky itself seemed to turn against them.

The sun did not disappear.

But for those living through AD 536, it may have felt that way.
