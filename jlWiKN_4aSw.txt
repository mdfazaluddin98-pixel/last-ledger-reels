She Made Her Son Emperor. He Ordered Her Death.

She helped put her son on Rome’s throne. Then he ordered her death.

Her name was Agrippina the Younger—sister of Caligula, wife of Emperor Claudius, and mother of Nero.

In AD 54, Nero became emperor at just sixteen. At first, Agrippina stood beside him, helping shape the new ruler and influencing the Roman court. But as Nero grew more powerful, his relationship with his mother began to collapse.

When Agrippina opposed his decisions and threatened his authority, Nero made a horrifying choice.

In AD 59, he arranged an attack that was supposed to kill her at sea. She survived the shipwreck—but Nero sent soldiers to finish what he had started.

The woman who helped create the emperor was now being hunted by him.

But how much of this story is fact? Ancient historians described poison, political manipulation, a staged shipwreck, and Agrippina’s final moments. Some details remain debated, and later accounts may have added dramatic elements.

This is the story of ambition, betrayal, and the dangerous relationship between a mother and the most powerful man in Rome.
